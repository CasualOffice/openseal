What the check tells you.

Four questions, answered by the verifier from the file alone. The verdict is the verifier's; this page never upgrades it.

  • {{icon:file-check}}IntegrityWhether the bytes each signature covers are exactly the bytes that were signed. One changed byte fails it.{{claim:tested|tampered fixtures in CI (verifier, pyHanko, EU DSS)|.github/workflows/ci.yml}}
  • {{icon:badge-check}}Certificate trustWhether the sealing certificate chains to a root you trust. Without your root, the answer is "could not be fully verified", never "valid".{{claim:deployment|which root you trust: the operator's sealing CA, or a public one it uses}}
  • {{icon:clock}}TimestampWhen an RFC 3161 timestamp authority says the signature existed, so the time does not rest on anyone's clock.{{claim:tested|the verifier's timestamp checks|crates/casual-sign-crypto/tests/checks.rs}}
  • {{icon:layers}}RevisionsEvery change appended after a signature, and whether it is one the signature allows (validation data, a document timestamp) or a change to what was signed.{{claim:tested|shadow and incremental-saving attack fixtures|crates/casual-sign-verify/tests/fixtures.rs}}

The full verifier in the web app also shows the audit trail attached to an OpenSeal PDF and a preview of its pages. The same check runs offline with openseal verify (download), and the seal is standard PAdES, so independent validators such as pyHanko and EU DSS read it too. A verdict here is a technical result, not a legal opinion.
