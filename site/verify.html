Verify

Verify a sealed PDF

Check whether a PDF is exactly what was signed, who sealed it, and when. The check runs in this browser with the same verifier the OpenSeal server and command line use, compiled to WebAssembly.

Your file is not uploaded. It is read in this tab and never leaves your device; this page sends nothing anywhere.

Drop a sealed PDF here

or choose one from your device. Up to 50 MB.

Trusted certificates (optional)

A signature's issuer can only be confirmed against a root you trust: your organisation's, or the signing service's. Add it as a DER or PEM file; it stays in this tab too.

No sealed PDF at hand? Try one of ours. Each is checked against the test root it was sealed under, which is for these samples only.

Or download them: sealed-agreement.pdf, tampered-agreement.pdf (one byte changed after sealing), and test-roots.pem.

What the check tells you.

Four questions, answered by the verifier from the file alone. The verdict is the verifier's; this page never upgrades it.

  • IntegrityWhether the bytes each signature covers are exactly the bytes that were signed. One changed byte fails it.Tested: tampered fixtures in CI (verifier, pyHanko, EU DSS)
  • Certificate trustWhether the sealing certificate chains to a root you trust. Without your root, the answer is "could not be fully verified", never "valid".Depends on your deployment: which root you trust: the operator's sealing CA, or a public one it uses
  • TimestampWhen an RFC 3161 timestamp authority says the signature existed, so the time does not rest on anyone's clock.Tested: the verifier's timestamp checks
  • RevisionsEvery change appended after a signature, and whether it is one the signature allows (validation data, a document timestamp) or a change to what was signed.Tested: shadow and incremental-saving attack fixtures

The full verifier in the web app also shows the audit trail attached to an OpenSeal PDF and a preview of its pages. The same check runs offline with openseal verify (download), and the seal is standard PAdES, so independent validators such as pyHanko and EU DSS read it too. A verdict here is a technical result, not a legal opinion.

Open-source electronic signatures under Apache-2.0. Part of CasualOffice.

Designed to support e-signature laws such as ESIGN, UETA and eIDAS. Nothing here is legal advice.
