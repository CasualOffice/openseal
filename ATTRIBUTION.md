OpenSeal is free to use, free to modify, free to embed and free to commercialize. This page explains what the
[Apache License 2.0](LICENSE) requires, and the one thing the project asks for as a courtesy.

- embedded SDKs and third-party integrations;
- modified, forked and redistributed products.

No commercial license, royalty, separate permission or product registration is needed. OpenSeal charges no fee for
its Apache-licensed software, and commercial and proprietary adoption is welcome.

## What the license requires

These are the Apache 2.0 conditions (section 4 of [LICENSE](LICENSE)), not project policy. When you redistribute
OpenSeal or a work derived from it, in source or binary form:

1. Give recipients a copy of the [LICENSE](LICENSE).
2. Keep the copyright, patent, trademark and attribution notices from the source you received.
3. Mark files you modified as changed.
4. Include the [NOTICE](NOTICE) file's attribution notices in a reasonable place: a `NOTICE` file shipped with your
   product, its documentation, or a third-party notices screen.

Running OpenSeal as a hosted service without distributing it does not trigger these conditions.

## What the project asks for (voluntary)

If OpenSeal helps you, please say so:

> **Powered by OpenSeal** — https://github.com/CasualOffice/openseal

Any reasonable place is fine: an application footer, an About dialog, a settings or acknowledgements page, your
documentation, your third-party notices, or an integration listing. "Built with OpenSeal" works too.

**This is a request, not a condition.** The Apache License does not require it, and leaving it out does not affect your
rights. [BRANDING.md](BRANDING.md) has ready-made text and logo snippets.

## Forks and modifications

Forks and derivative products are welcome under Apache 2.0. Keep the legally required notices listed above. You do not
need to keep OpenSeal's name or logo in your user interface, and you may give your fork its own name.

## Names and logos

You may refer to OpenSeal accurately, for example "Powered by OpenSeal" or "Built with OpenSeal", or "compatible with
OpenSeal's API". Using OpenSeal does not mean your product is endorsed, certified, sponsored or maintained by the
OpenSeal project or CasualOffice. Please don't present it that way, and don't use the OpenSeal name or logo as the name
or logo of your own product. Section 6 of the Apache License already says the license grants no trademark rights beyond
describing where the work came from.
